# Genial Relations — Product Spec v0.1

Oct 19, 2026 · @Madhura Redij

## Overview

Genial Relations is a hypertext story set at a family reunion of words. All 25 guests descend from one ancient root, \*gene- ("give birth, beget"). Readers drop in at any of five tables, follow gossip and family ties from word to word, collect evidence, and decide whether King, at the head of the table, is really kin. v1 is one family and one pre-written story; later versions build new families from the same system. All guest content lives in [Genial Relations — Content v0.1](https://claude.ai/artifact/A6mZEBktKM5aegJhJcn1v8).

**Why this project**

- First build with Claude Code: AI writes the code and teaches along the way.
- Small, self-contained, shareable in public.
- Picked against four criteria: finishable in 2–3 weeks; a hook people forward; in Madhura's voice (wit, language, etymology, craft); shows interaction and motion craft.

**Ground rules**

1. Every word fact is checked against a source before it appears. Etymonline is the source of record for v1.
2. The record is fixed; the gossip is generated. Generation may invent dialogue, motive and mood, never dates, routes or relations.
3. Creative and conceptual decisions are Madhura's. Claude offers options.

The title is Genial Relations (renamed from the working title "Word Birthdays" on 2026-10-23).

## Core concept

Words are characters; a root is their family; the reader is a guest at the reunion.

- **Root as family.** One Proto-Indo-European root (the reconstructed ancestor of English, Hindi, Marathi and many others) branches into English words that look nothing alike.
- **Tables by route.** Guests sit by how they entered English: Old English, Old French, Latin, later French, German. Each table also has a personality and an event.
- **Two layers on every page.**
  - *The record*: sourced, fixed facts in our own words. Root, route, first use in English, meaning changes, relatives.
  - *The gossip*: invented scenes, dialogue and testimony built on the record, always shown in the gossip hand.
- **Hypertext.** Pages link through blood (etymology, in ink) and gossip (the story, in pencil). There is no single reading order.
- **The mystery.** Is King kin? Every table holds a step of his line back to \*gene-, every table has to be visited, and the verdict is delivered at the head table.
- **The reader's trail.** Clues pin themselves to the evidence row as you find them. The finished row is your trail card.

**Bench (v2):** native, nation, genre, renaissance, nee.

## Story system

The genre is a comedy of manners with one running mystery: **is King kin?** Each table is a self-contained comic scene, so the story works in any reading order.

**The mystery: is King kin?** King sits at the head chair and claims to be family. The reader tests the claim by tracing his line back to \*gene-, one step at a time. The record supports **not proven**: Etymonline lists King under \*gene-, but his own entry calls his origin uncertain and his link to *cynn* "possibly". Gingerly, Germ, Genie and Kriss Kringle are ordinary guests. The head-table host is **Kindred** (formerly Kin).

**The ancestor.** \*gene- is a silent portrait in the hall. Its page holds the record only: the root's meaning and what the asterisk means. No one speaks for it.

**The steps.** Six steps make King's case: the chain King ← *cyning* ← *kuningaz*, the doubtful "possibly" link to *cynn*, *cynn* ← \*gene-, the rival "noble birth" story, and the sound shift that turned g into k. Five are found through tables; one sits only in a record, with no snippet pointing to it. The full list, with sources, is in the content doc.

**Rumours and decoys.** At the Latin and French tables a step arrives as gossip about King, a rumour until a record checks it. A few rumours are true look-alikes rather than links: *Kringle* comes from German *Christkind'l*, "little Christ child", not from king.

**Auto-pinning.** Opening a guest's page that holds a clue pins it to the evidence row, with no choosing. Steps pin on torn paper; rumours pin on lined pencil paper. Opening the page whose record settles a rumour flips it to confirmed, or strikes it through as "only sounds alike".

**Clue structure: pointer + split**

1. A table's snippet hints at the guest who holds a step. Hints can be vague or clear.
2. Opening that guest's page pins the step.
3. At the Latin and French tables the step is gossip, a rumour until a record checks it.
4. Some word pages lead to guests at other tables. These are leads, never requirements.

**Verdict and endings.** Once all five tables are visited, the verdict card shows the case file, every pinned scrap in the order found, and asks **Is King kin?** (*Kin / Not kin / Not proven*). The reader reads the case and answers. Then:

- **Ending 1, Not proven:** Kindred reads the record aloud. King's papers say *possibly*; his link to kin is undetermined, but hardly doubted. King keeps the head chair and pays for dinner.
- **Ending 6, You:** always follows. The last place card has the reader's name on it, because everyone who reads English is related to every guest. It leads to the trail card.

There's no score and no losing: readers see how far they agree with the record.

**Words are characters.** Personality comes from the record: Puny is literally "born later" and stuck at the kids' table; Genteel has been used mostly in mockery since the 1840s; Pregnant meant "convincing" before she meant "expecting."

**Tone.** A dry family comedy where every joke rests on a fact. No jokes about religion or bodies, no invented facts. Full rules are in the content doc's tone guide.

**Life events → word processes** (the bigger vision; v1 uses the reunion)

| Event | Word process |
| --- | --- |
| Birth | First recorded use in English |
| Family reunion | Cognates: words sharing a root |
| Wedding | Compounds and blends |
| Separated at birth | Doublets: one ancestor, two routes into English |
| Immigration, adoption | Loanwords |
| Name change | Semantic shift |
| Black sheep / social climber | Pejoration / amelioration |
| Death | Obsolete words |
| Family rumours | Folk etymology |

## Data model

Every guest and every table is a record with the same fields: a *schema*, a fixed shape every entry fills in. In v1 the records live as plain data files (JSON) in the project, so there's no database or live service to run. The same schema later feeds new families.

**Guest record**

| Field | Holds | Example: puny |
| --- | --- | --- |
| word | The word | puny |
| table | Where they sit | back-with-an-accent |
| role | host / guest / floater | guest |
| route, first\_use | How and when it entered English | French, 1570s |
| senses | Meanings over time, oldest first | inferior in rank → small, weak |
| record\_text | The record, in our own words | (from content doc) |
| gossip\_text | The gossip paragraph | (from content doc) |
| testimony | Their clue | a step or rumour about King, if they hold one |
| suspect | Is this guest the suspect? (only King) | false |
| links | Typed links: blood / gossip / across the room | nature, naive · wunderkind |
| object | Their marginalia object | an oversized judge's wig |
| type\_tag | Typography Layer 2 tag | small |
| sources | URL for every fact | Etymonline entry |
| status | checked / to check | checked |

**Table record:** name, route, event, guest list, setup lines, and pointer lines per guest.

**Floater record:** the guest fields plus cameo lines and the tables where they appear.

**Snippet rules (build-time generation)**

1. Each visit assembles a snippet: one setup line + one pointer line for a guest not yet visited + a floater cameo about one visit in three.
2. Never repeat a pointer line twice in a row; never point back to the page you came from.
3. All lines are written and checked in advance. Pointer lines carry gossip only, never new facts.
4. Words on the exclusion list never appear.

*Build-time generation* means the variety comes from recombining lines that already exist. *Runtime generation* (writing new lines live with an AI call) is a v2 option, with costs: money, delay, and facts nobody has checked.

## UX

The reader can enter anywhere, has to visit every table, and leaves with their own trail.

**Page types**

- **Landing page (the reunion hall).** All five tables show at once: one phone screen, or a wide room on desktop. Each guest's place card sits around their table's rim, and a small invitation card lies on the head table. Floaters are words on torn paper, scattered at random between the tables; tapping one opens a paper card with their line and a link. A portrait of \*gene-, the absent ancestor, hangs in the hall and opens its record. Below: the full invitation and the evidence row.
- **Table page.** A round table seen from above, place cards around the edge, and an overheard snippet in the centre. Cards you've read get a red crayon zigzag. All snippet text stays inside the table, clear of the cards. The head table uses the same layout; once all five tables are cleared, its centre becomes the verdict card.
- **Word page.** One format, the character page, in this order: the name drawn with numbered callouts, the record (meanings), the gossip, the pinned testimony, then relatives drawn as place cards joined to the word (navy line = blood, blue wavy line = gossip) with a lead across the room. No section labels.

**Doors in**

- The landing page: any object, floater, or table.
- An invitation from a friend lands you on a specific word page.

**Evidence**

- Clues pin themselves: opening a guest's page that holds a step or a rumour adds it to the evidence row.
- Rumours stay on pencil paper until a record settles them: confirmed, or struck through as "only sounds alike".
- Every word page links back to its table and to at least one other page. Before launch, check the link map as a graph: no orphan pages, no required order.

**Things to send**

- **The trail card:** the margin with every evidence piece you collected, your path in red pencil, and your verdict stamped in the corner.
- **The invitation:** "Someone there isn't who they say they are. I think you'll like Puny." The guest named is the last page the sender visited.
- Both need a *link preview*: the image a chat app shows when you paste a link.

**Constraints**

- Phone first at 390px, with a 1440px desktop layout for every screen. Desktop pages fit one viewport (1440×900) except the hall.
- No accounts. Progress is kept in the browser and can be lost.
- Motion respects the reduced-motion setting: cards settling, the snippet appearing, evidence flying to the margin.
- Accessibility: every margin object and place card has a text label; ink, pencil and red are told apart by style as well as colour.

## Visual direction

Hand-made paper party: off-white crumpled paper, black ink, grey pencil, navy drawings and bright crayon marks. The mockups on the Screens canvas are the reference.

| Element | Look |
| --- | --- |
| Paper | Off-white #F7F4EE with a crumpled texture |
| Text | All black (#141414); pencil text is textured grey #6E6E6E in Reenie Beanie |
| Drawings | Navy #23306E line, wobbly and organic: tables, objects, fasteners |
| Marks | Crayon texture; the shape carries the meaning, colours rotate (red, green, blue, yellow, orange) |
| Place cards | Folded paper with a back flap and shadow, name only, no symbols |
| Tables | Slightly oval, no chairs, each one different in shape, tilt and setting |
| Evidence | Torn paper pinned with a pushpin, thumbtack or nail (varied by place); uncollected pieces are faded scraps |

**Marks**

- Zigzag: your trail, a visited card, the page you're on.
- Circle: a record fact, a numbered callout, a chosen verdict.
- Wavy underline: gossip, a pointer.
- Question mark: the suspect (King only).
- Manicule: the snippet points here.

**Rules**

- No drawn figures or creatures; the manicule is the one hand allowed.
- No explanatory labels ("Evidence", "Testimony", "Relatives"); the objects and grouping carry the meaning.
- Words on one table vary in size, weight and style within their era font.
- Marginalia are objects with expressive faces, drawn by Madhura; dashed `[object · guest]` placeholders until then.

## Typography system

The type system has three layers, and only the first one changes the font. Era picks a font family; meaning tags adjust size, case and spacing; variable axes add character computed from the record. Puny doesn't need its own font: it's set smaller. Body text is Inconsolata and the pencil hand is Reenie Beanie; only a word's name gets era treatment.

**Layer 1: era → font family** (computed from first\_use)

| Era band | Font | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old English | Jacquard 24 (pixel blackletter) | chosen 2026-10-22 |
| 1200–1499 | EB Garamond | placeholder |
| 1500–1699 | IM Fell English | chosen |
| 1800–1899 | Bodoni Moda | chosen |
| 1900 onward | Fraunces | chosen |

Five bands keep the set small enough to look like one book. All are Google Fonts; check each licence before launch.

**Layer 2: meaning tags → treatment** (assigned per word from a short, fixed list)

| Tag | Treatment | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| small | Smaller size | puny |
| grand | Larger size, capitals | king |
| gentle | Lighter weight, wider spacing | gentle, genteel |
| plain | Lowercase, no flourish | naive, kind |
| contested | Slanted or wonky forms | king |
| obsolete | Faded ink + dagger | (none yet in v1) |

A fixed list keeps the system scalable: generation can suggest a tag, but it can't invent one. Madhura approves tags.

**Layer 3: variable axes** (computed from the record, used where the era font is variable)

*Variable font* means one font file with continuous sliders such as weight and width. Fraunces has four axes: weight, optical size, softness (SOFT) and wonky (WONK).

| Axis | Driven by | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight | Number of relatives | Big families look heavier |
| WONK (on/off) | Has a disputed origin | A binary axis for a yes/no fact |
| SOFT | Meaning improved vs. soured over time | Softer for amelioration, sharper for pejoration |
| Optical size | Automatic with size | Leave to the font |

Most era fonts are static, so in v1 the axes only apply in the 1900+ band. See options below.

**Rules for combining**

1. Era sets the family. Tags and axes never change it.
2. Tags change at most two properties each.
3. Minimum size and contrast hold for every word, so tags never make a name unreadable.
4. The record decides; nothing is chosen by hand except the tag.

**Pixel Surplus fonts.** Their freebie licence (seen in a third-party copy, not on their site) limits use to projects with no business or monetary gain, and lists *embedding in apps or websites* as a separate licence. Embedding a font on the live site probably needs that paid licence. Using it in an image (a trail card made in Figma) may fall under personal use, but a portfolio piece could count as career-related. Check the licence file that ships with each font before using one.

**Options for Layer 3**

- **A. Axes only where available.** Honest to era; the variable effect shows up only on modern words.
- **B. One variable face for every name.** Use Fraunces for all names; era shows through ornament and borders instead. Fully scalable, but loses the era fonts.
- **C. Era fonts for names, variable face for the gossip hand.** The gossip layer gets the character; names keep their era.

## Production

**Content workflow.** Claude drafts from the record → Madhura edits → facts are re-checked against the source → the page is approved and goes into the data files. The content doc is the working draft.

**Source usage.** Etymonline is the source of record. Record text is written in our own words with a link to the entry; entries are never copied.

**Tech and hosting.** Decided in CLAUDE.md: Next.js, TypeScript, CSS Modules, JSON data validated with Zod, Motion, localStorage, Vercel.
